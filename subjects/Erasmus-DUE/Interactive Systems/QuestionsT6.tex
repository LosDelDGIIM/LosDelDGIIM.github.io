\section{Theories and Frameworks for Design}


\begin{ejercicio}
    Explain the main concepts of Activity Theory.

    Activity Theory is a framework for understanding human activity and its relationship to the environment. The main concepts include:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \ul{Activity}: A goal-directed action that involves a subject (the person performing the activity) and an object (the goal or target of the activity).
        \item \ul{Action}: The specific steps taken by the subject to achieve the object.
        \item \ul{Operation}: The technical or practical aspects of the action, including the tools and resources used. It uses instruments to mediate the interaction between the subject and the object.
    \end{itemize}

    Three important aspects of Activity Theory are:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \ul{Subject}: The individual or group performing the activity, who is influenced by their personal characteristics, experiences, and motivations.
        \item \ul{Object}: The goal or target of the activity, which can be a physical object, a task, or a social outcome.
        \item \ul{Instruments}: The tools, resources, and technologies used by the subject to achieve the object, which can include physical tools, digital technologies, or social practices.
    \end{itemize}

    Lastly, three more important aspects of Activity Theory are:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \ul{Community}: The social context in which the activity takes place, including the norms, rules, and relationships that shape the activity.
        \item \ul{Rules}: The formal and informal guidelines that govern the activity, including laws, regulations, and social norms.
        \item \ul{Division of Labor}: The distribution of tasks and responsibilities among the members of the community, which can influence the efficiency and effectiveness of the activity.
    \end{itemize}
\end{ejercicio}

\begin{ejercicio}
    Give an explanation of the concept of internal and external attribution according to the Attribution Theory and provide an example for each in the context of HCI.

    Attribution Theory is a psychological framework that explains how individuals interpret and assign causes to events and behaviors.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \ul{Internal Attribution}: This occurs when an individual attributes the cause of an event or behavior to internal factors, such as their own abilities, personality traits, or effort. For example, in the context of HCI, if a user successfully completes a task using a new software application, they may attribute their success to their own skills and knowledge.
        \item \ul{External Attribution}: This occurs when an individual attributes the cause of an event or behavior to external factors, such as the environment, other people, or situational circumstances. For example, in the context of HCI, if a user struggles to complete a task due to a poorly designed user interface, they may attribute their difficulty to the design of the software rather than their own abilities.
    \end{itemize}
\end{ejercicio}

    \begin{itemize}
        \item You take part in a team competition and end up losing.
        
        An internal stable attribution would be that you are not skilled enough to win, while an internal variable attribution could be that you did not prepare enough for the competition.

\begin{ejercicio}
    Explain the three conditions of the Self-Determination Theory and give an example of how to foster each of them in the context of HCI.

    The three conditions of the Self-Determination Theory are:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item \ul{Autonomy}: The need to feel in control of one's own actions and decisions. In the context of HCI, autonomy can be fostered by letting users make their own decisions instead of forcing them to follow a specific path. For example, providing users with customizable settings and options in a software application allows them to tailor the experience to their preferences.
        \item \ul{Competence}: The need to feel effective and capable in one's actions. In HCI, competence can be fostered by providing users with clear feedback and guidance on how to use a system effectively. For example, including tutorials, tooltips, and progress indicators can help users understand how to navigate and utilize the features of an application, thereby enhancing their sense of competence.
        \item \ul{Relatedness}: The need to feel connected to others and have a sense of belonging. In HCI, relatedness can be fostered by incorporating social features that allow users to interact and collaborate with others. For example, integrating social media sharing options, discussion forums, or collaborative tools within an application can help users feel connected to a community and enhance their overall experience.
    \end{itemize}
\end{ejercicio}
