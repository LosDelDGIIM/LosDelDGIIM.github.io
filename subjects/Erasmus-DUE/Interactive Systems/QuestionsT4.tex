\begin{ejercicio}
    Describe the concept of ``Reality-Virtuality Continuum''.

    The Reality-Virtuality Continuum is a spectrum that represents the range of experiences between the real world and a fully virtual environment. It the edges of the continuum are defined by two extremes:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \ul{Real Environment}: This represents the physical world that we experience through our senses, where we interact with tangible objects and people.
        \item \ul{Virtual Environment}: This represents a completely computer-generated environment that immerses users in a simulated world.
    \end{enumerate}

    Between these two extremes, there are various mixed reality experiences that combine elements of both the real and virtual worlds. These experiences can be categorized into two main types:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \ul{Augmented Reality (AR)}: This involves overlaying digital content onto the real world, enhancing the user's perception and interaction with their environment. AR allows users to see and interact with virtual objects while still being aware of the real world around them.
        \item \ul{Augmented Virtuality (AV)}: This involves incorporating real-world elements into a virtual environment, allowing users to interact with both virtual and real-world objects within a simulated space. AV can provide a more immersive experience by blending real-world elements with virtual content.
    \end{enumerate}
    The Reality-Virtuality Continuum highlights the range of experiences that can be created by combining real and virtual elements, allowing for a diverse array of applications in fields such as gaming, education, training, and entertainment.
\end{ejercicio}

\begin{ejercicio}
    Name one of the key differences between Video See-Through (VST) and Optical See-Through (OST). Explain two advantages and two disadvantages of each. Name one scenario for which each would fit best.\\

    One of the key differences between Video See-Through (VST) and Optical See-Through (OST) is the way they present the real world to the user. VST uses cameras to capture the real world and displays it on a screen, while OST allows users to see the real world directly through transparent displays or lenses.

    Regarding the advantages and disadvantages of each technology:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \ul{Video See-Through (VST)}: These systems allow more complex visual effects (as they can use devices with more processing power) and cheaper devices (as they can use general-purpose devices such as smartphones). However, they have a lower field of view and a higher latency (as the video feed needs to be processed before being displayed). A scenario where VST would fit best is in a mobile AR application for interior design, where users can visualize furniture and decor in their homes using their smartphones.
        \item \ul{Optical See-Through (OST)}: These systems allow a higher field of view and a more immersive experience (as users can see the real world directly). However, they have a lower visual quality (as the virtual content is overlaid on the real world) and a higher cost (as they require specialized hardware). A scenario where OST would fit best is in an industrial AR application for maintenance and repair, where technicians can see virtual instructions overlaid on machinery while working hands-free.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{ejercicio}

\begin{ejercicio}
    During the lecture, you learned about model-free and model-based tracking. Briefly describe how these tracking approaches work, highlight the differences, and give an example application in a real-world scenario for each of these tracking approaches.

    Model-free tracking relies on detecting and tracking features in the environment without prior knowledge of the scene. It uses computer vision techniques to identify and follow points of interest, such as edges, corners, or textures, in real-time. An example application of model-free tracking is in AR gaming, where the system tracks the user's movements and interactions with virtual objects in a dynamic environment without requiring a pre-defined model.

    Model-based tracking, on the other hand, uses a pre-defined 3D model of the environment or objects to track their position and orientation. It compares the observed features in the camera feed with the known model to estimate the object's pose. An example application of model-based tracking is in AR navigation, where the system uses a 3D map of a building to guide users through complex indoor spaces by overlaying directional arrows and information on their device's display.
\end{ejercicio}

\begin{ejercicio}
    Explain the concepts of ``markers'' and ``SLAM'' in AR. How do they differ and which technique (model-based or model-free) do each of them use?

    In AR, ``markers'' are small objects or patterns that are placed in the environment to serve as reference points for a (usually stationary) model-based tracking system. They allow the AR system to recognize and track the position and orientation of the markers, enabling the overlay of virtual content onto the real world. 
    
    SLAM (Simultaneous Localization and Mapping) is a model-free tracking technique that allows an AR system to build a map of the environment while simultaneously tracking the user's position within that environment. It uses computer vision algorithms to detect and track features in the scene, enabling the system to understand the spatial relationships between objects and the user's movements.
\end{ejercicio}

\begin{ejercicio}
    Explain the concepts of tracking and registration in Augmented Reality, detailing their role in overlaying virtual content onto the real world. Differentiate between geometric and photometric registration, providing a brief example for each. Why is low latency crucial for a good AR experience?

    Tracking in AR refers to the process of determining the position and orientation of the user or objects in the real world. Registration is the process of aligning virtual content with the real world based on the tracked information.
    
    Geometric registration involves aligning virtual objects with the physical environment based on their spatial relationships, while photometric registration also considers the lighting and color properties of the scene to ensure that virtual content blends seamlessly with the real world. An example of geometric registration is placing a virtual chair in a room so that it appears to be sitting on the floor, while an example of photometric registration is adjusting the brightness and color of a virtual object to match the lighting conditions of the room.
    
    Lastly, low latency is crucial for a good AR experience because any delay between the user's movements and the corresponding updates in the virtual content can lead to disorientation, motion sickness, and a less immersive experience.
\end{ejercicio}

\begin{ejercicio}
    Define what situated visualization in AR means, contrast it with conventional visualization methods, and provide two different techniques used for situated visualization along with an example use case for each.

    Conventional visualization methods typically involve just displaying the information and the virtual content, while situated visualization in AR involves integrating the information and virtual content into the user's physical environment, allowing for a more immersive and context-aware experience. Two techniques used for situated visualization are:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \ul{X-ray vision}: This technique allows users to see through physical objects to visualize hidden structures or components. An example use case is in medical training, where students can use AR to see the internal anatomy of a patient while performing a simulated surgery.
        \item \ul{Annotations and labels}: This technique involves overlaying text or graphical information directly onto real-world objects to provide context or additional information. An example use case is in industrial maintenance, where technicians can use AR to see labels and instructions overlaid on machinery, guiding them through repair procedures.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{ejercicio}


\begin{ejercicio}
    List and explain two input modalities that can be useful to interact in AR. Name one advantage and one disadvantage for each.

    Two input modalities that can be useful to interact in AR are:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \ul{Gaze tracking}: This modality uses eye-tracking technology to determine where the user is looking, allowing for hands-free interaction with virtual content. An advantage of gaze tracking is that it enables intuitive and natural interaction, as users can simply look at objects to select or manipulate them. A disadvantage is that it may require specialized hardware and calibration, which can be costly and time-consuming.
        \item \ul{Gesture recognition}: This modality uses cameras or sensors to detect and interpret the user's hand or body movements as input commands. An advantage of gesture recognition is that it allows for immersive and engaging interaction, as users can physically interact with virtual objects. A disadvantage is that it requires users to perform specific gestures, which may not be suitable for all users that need their hands free or have limited mobility.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{ejercicio}

\begin{ejercicio}
    During the lecture, gestural interaction techniques for AR were discussed. Explain two advantages and two disadvantages of using gestures as an input modality in AR.

    Two advantages of using gestures as an input modality in AR are:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \ul{Avoid the Midas Touch problem}: Gestures allow users to interact with virtual content assuring that they are not unintentionally triggering actions, as the system can be designed to recognize specific gestures for interaction.
        \item \ul{Avoid the need for touching physical devices}: Gestures enable users to interact with virtual content without the need for physical contact, which can be beneficial in scenarios where hygiene or accessibility is a concern.
    \end{enumerate}

    Two disadvantages of using gestures as an input modality in AR are:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item \ul{Limited precision}: Gestures may not provide the same level of precision as other input modalities, such as touch or stylus input, which can be a limitation for tasks that require fine control or accuracy.
        \item \ul{Fatigue}: Prolonged use of gestures can lead to physical fatigue, especially for users who need to perform repetitive or complex movements, which can impact the overall user experience and limit the duration of interaction.
    \end{enumerate}
\end{ejercicio}


\begin{ejercicio}
    Explain the ``Midas Touch'' problem in AR. How can it be mitigated?

    The ``Midas Touch'' problem in AR refers to the issue of unintentional interactions with virtual content when just using the gaze as an input modality. Since the system may interpret the user's gaze as a command to interact with virtual objects, it can lead to accidental selections or actions, disrupting the user experience.

    In order to mitigate the Midas Touch problem, gaze can be combined with other input modalities, such as gestures or voice commands, to confirm the user's intent before executing an action. For example, a user could gaze at an object to select it and then perform a specific gesture or say a command to confirm the selection. This approach helps ensure that interactions are intentional and reduces the likelihood of accidental actions.
\end{ejercicio}
